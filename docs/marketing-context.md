# Marketing context — Shruti Gupta portfolio

## ICP
Content/marketing leads at SaaS, AI-tool, and e-commerce companies + agency account leads staffing content. They buy freelance SEO content writing — blogs, landing pages, product/tool content, editing.

## Top 3 pains (from customer-psychology.md)
1. AI-flavored mush from "SEO writers" — editing costs more than writing.
2. Writers who don't understand the product — shallow, marketing-flavored copy.
3. Unreliable freelancers — slipped deadlines, quiet inboxes.

## The wedge
Shruti = **niche depth (SaaS/AI/crypto/e-commerce) + technical literacy (MERN, REST APIs — she reads what she writes about) + proof (20+ brands, 6 yrs, 60% CTR lift)**. Most writer portfolios assert "quality"; this one demonstrates understanding. The site itself is built as a writing sample — editorial design, crisp structure.

## Alternatives map
| Alternative | Why it fails them |
|---|---|
| Content mills | Cheap, generic, uneditable output |
| AI-only writing | Volume without intent-match or voice |
| Generalist freelancer | Nice prose, no SEO, doesn't get tech |
| Big agency | Quality but expensive and slow |

## One-line value prop
**SEO content that ranks and reads like a human wrote it — for SaaS, AI, and tech brands.**

## Voice lock (for all copy)
- **Formality:** confident professional, first-person "I" — warm but precise; editor's voice (she edits too).
- **Do:** short declaratives, concrete nouns, numbers with sources, "publish-ready", "search intent", "SaaS", "how-to", "landing page".
- **Banned:** "passionate", "delve", "leverage", "synergy", "unlock", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced world", "I am writing to express".
- **Example in-voice:** "Six years, twenty brands, one habit: drafts that arrive ready to publish."

## Conversion goal
Single conversion: **email inquiry** (mailto CTA everywhere; no form, no tracking by default).
