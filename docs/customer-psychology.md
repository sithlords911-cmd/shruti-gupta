# Customer psychology — who hires Shruti (understand-customers brief)

## 1. Core identity & role
- **Primary ICP:** Content marketing manager / head of content at a SaaS, AI-tool, or e-commerce company (often seed–Series B, small team, big content targets). Secondary: agency account lead (like DesigningBrain) staffing client content; founder doing marketing themselves.
- **Their real goal:** Publish content that actually ranks and converts — on deadline, without hand-holding — so their pipeline/traffic numbers move and they look good internally.

## 2. Deepest pains (the nightmare)
- **"I briefed a writer and got AI-flavored mush."** The #1 pain of 2025-26: every freelancer says "SEO content" and delivers rehashed SERP-top-10 summaries or raw ChatGPT output. Editing it takes longer than writing it themselves.
- **"They don't get the product."** Writers who can't read a feature list, API doc, or product UI produce shallow copy. SaaS/AI buyers especially feel this — the content reads like marketing, not understanding.
- **"Deadlines slip, communication goes quiet."** The freelancer who vanishes mid-month.
- **"Traffic is flat, leadership asks why."** Inner monologue: *If this next batch doesn't move impressions/CTR, it's on me. I need a safe pair of hands, not another gamble.*
- **What they tried:** content mills (cheap, unusable), AI-only workflows (volume without rank or voice), generalist freelancers (nice prose, zero SEO), expensive agencies (good but ₹₹₹ and slow).

## 3. Unspoken desires (the magic wand)
- A writer who sends publish-ready drafts: right intent match, right structure, right internal-link moments — first draft ≈ final draft.
- Someone who reads the product brief/PRD and asks two smart questions — not ten basic ones.
- Evidence they can show their boss: "this article hit position 3 / CTR up X%."
- The cheat-code feeling: *agency-grade output at freelance rates, who also understands our stack.*

## 4. Language & worldview (their vocabulary — reuse these words)
- "search intent", "SERP", "E-E-A-T", "topical authority", "CTR", "conversion copy", "on-page", "refresh vs net-new", "SME input", "edit cycle", "brief", "WordPress", "GSC impressions".
- Core belief to affirm: **AI can draft, but ranking + trust still needs a human who researches and understands the product.** Another: good SEO writing reads like good writing, not keyword stuffing.

## Blitz-copy angles
- **Identity trigger:** "for teams whose content has to rank and read like a human wrote it."
- **Emotional hook:** publish-ready drafts — no third edit, no "does she even know what our product does".
- **Pattern interrupt:** a writer's site that itself is perfectly written and structured — the site IS the sample.
- **Dopamine payoff:** named brands, real numbers (60% CTR lift, 20+ brands, 6 years), and proof she can read code docs.
